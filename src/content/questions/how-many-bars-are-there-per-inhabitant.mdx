<Method>

All of it is Spain. A bar here is a premises whose main activity the business
register files under CNAE 563, *establecimientos de bebidas*, meaning drinking
places. Not restaurants, which are 561, and not catering, which is 562. The register counts premises, so
a bar that changes hands and keeps trading is not a closure, and one that shuts
and reopens as a restaurant is.

Both series are read on **1 January**: the register reports its stock on that
date, and the population figure is the reading for the same day. Dividing a
stock of premises by a population measured six months later would give a
different answer for no reason but the calendar.

The 7.2 a day is a net figure across fifteen years: closures minus openings,
divided by the days between the two readings. It is not a count of shutters
coming down.

Marks on the second figure are rounded thousands, which is what makes them
countable. The exact counts are in the article and the change by region is in
the data behind it.

`npm run data:bars` rebuilds `src/data/bars.json` from the raw API responses
kept in `data/source/`. It refuses to write if any figure quoted here has moved.

</Method>
