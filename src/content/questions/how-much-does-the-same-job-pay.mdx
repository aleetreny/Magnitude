"What does a cook earn?" has no answer. Spain's statistics office publishes five
figures for each trade rather than one, and the distance between the first and
the last is the part everybody leaves out.

Below, every occupation is a curve: horizontally, where you stand inside your
own trade, from worst to best paid; vertically, what you take home. **The slope
is the inequality.** Drag across the chart.

The two extremes make the case on their own. In *cultura y espectáculo* the
bottom tenth earns €10,098; in *informática y TIC*, €25,979 — two and a half
times more. At the top the two trades almost meet: €61,458 against €67,741,
a difference of a tenth. One profession fans out by a factor of six, the other
by two and a half.

So the ranking you get depends on where you read it. Sort these trades by their
median and you get one league table; sort them by what the bottom tenth takes
home and a fifth of the pairs change places. Both tables are true. Neither is
"what the job pays".

### Method

The INE publishes the mean and the 10th, 25th, 50th, 75th and 90th percentiles
of gross annual pay for each sub-major group of the CNO-11 — the finest level at
which a wage distribution exists. Anyone showing you "the airline pilot's salary"
is showing you the parent group relabelled.

The curve between those five points is a monotone (PCHIP) interpolation of the
quantile function in log-salary, so it passes exactly through every published
percentile and cannot overshoot between them; a check in the build refuses the
dataset if any reconstructed percentile drifts from the published one. Outside
p10–p90 nothing is published, and the tails simply continue the slope of the
outermost measured pair. Those stretches are drawn dashed on a shaded ground —
real wage tails are fatter than any smooth extrapolation, so treat the top of
each curve as a floor rather than an estimate.

A **†** marks the trades whose estimate the INE flags as resting on 100–500
observations. The survey covers employees only, so the self-employed, domestic
work and military occupations are absent.
