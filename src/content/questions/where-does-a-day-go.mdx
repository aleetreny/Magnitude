Eighteen European countries kept diaries. The days came back almost identical.

Five threads, and none of them bends by more than an hour and three quarters
across the whole of Europe. No block of the day is more than **1h36** wider in
one country than in another: that widest gap is free time, between Finland and
Romania. The narrowest is sleep, **42 minutes** between Europe's shortest night
and its longest.

The one block that follows a map is the table. The six countries whose middle
lies furthest south all pass **1h45** there. Of the six furthest north, only the
Netherlands does.

<Method>

All eighteen countries are in Europe, and every figure is the average day of a
person **aged 20 to 74**, counted across every day of the week, weekends
included. That is why nobody appears to work eight hours: the eight-hour day of
the people who have one, spread over seven days and over everyone in the age
band, comes to about three.

The survey is Eurostat's harmonised time use survey, in which people write down
what they are doing through a day. It is published in **rounds rather than
years**, and the 2010 round is the most recent one in the table. A day in that
round is not a day now, and nothing here should be read as current.

Five of the six blocks come straight off the table: sleep, eating, employment
and study, household and family care, and leisure. The sixth, **rest**, is what
is left of the twenty-four hours once the other five are counted. It holds
washing and dressing, every journey that is not the journey to work, and the
minutes a diary left unclassified. Taking it as a residual is what makes every
row exactly one day long, so two rows can be compared by eye.

The United Kingdom's rest block is the widest, **3h06** against a median of
**2h12** everywhere else. Its diaries recorded more travel and left far more time
unclassified: 46 minutes against 2 in Spain. The five named blocks are read off
the table either way.

Furthest south and furthest north are measured at the centre of a country's
mainland, not of everything it governs: France's centre of gravity including
its overseas departments sits in the Atlantic.

`npm run data:time-use` rebuilds `src/data/time-use.json` from the response kept
in `data/source/`. It refuses to write if any figure quoted here has moved.

</Method>
