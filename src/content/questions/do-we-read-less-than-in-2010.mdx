"Nobody reads any more" is a claim about a trend, so it needs a line, not an
anecdote. The measurable version: of every hundred adults, how many finished at
least one book in the previous twelve months?

Fifty-eight down to fifty. That is a real decline, and it is also a much duller
one than the phrase "the death of reading" implies: over fourteen years, roughly
half a point a year.

<MarginNote>

The interesting part is the bump. 2020 interrupts the trend and then hands it
straight back — by 2022 the line is below where it would have been without the
interruption. Whatever made people read that year, it was the empty calendar,
not a change of habit.

A caution about what this measures: "at least one book" is a very low bar, and a
population can clear it in the same numbers while reading far less. This line
counts readers, not reading. The question of how much time those readers spend
is a different question, and a different chart.

### Method

Self-reported participation, adults aged 16 and over, twelve-month recall.
Self-reported reading is generous — people round up, and the rounding is
probably stable across years, which is what makes the *shape* more trustworthy
than the *level*.

The numbers above are **illustrative sample data shipped with this template**.
Before publishing, replace them with a real series — national participation
surveys and [Eurostat's culture statistics](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/culture)
both publish one, though on different definitions, so pick one and stay with it.
