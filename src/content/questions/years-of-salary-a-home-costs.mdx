The usual way to ask about house prices is to ask what they cost. That number is
useless on its own: it means nothing without the salary standing next to it. So
the question is better put in years. How long would a person on the local median
wage have to work — spending nothing at all — to pay for a 70m² flat in the city
they already live in?

The spread is the finding. A flat in Paris costs nearly twice what the same flat
costs in Rome, measured in the only currency that matters to the person buying
it — their own working life. And the two cities at the top of the list get there
by opposite routes: Paris is expensive, while Lisbon is not especially expensive
so much as underpaid.

That last point is the one the headline price always hides. Two cities can print
the same price per square metre and sit years apart on this chart, because the
denominator moved and nobody photographed it.

### Method

Each bar divides the median asking price of a 70m² new-build home by the gross
median annual salary in the same city, both for the same year. Gross, not net:
tax treatment varies enough across these countries that net figures would
compare tax codes as much as housing markets. Nothing is discounted for
mortgage interest, deposits or transaction costs, all of which push the real
figure higher.
